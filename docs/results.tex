\begin{abstract}
Notes on a set of proposed machine-learning improvements to MADGRAV, a blind
gravitational-wave anomaly search \citep{Inguglia2026}. We reimplement the stage-1 and
stage-2 front end, which the upstream release does not distribute, and evaluate every
proposed change at fixed false-alarm rate against time-slide background pushed through
the same selection as the foreground.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and constraints}

Five constraints are inherited from the upstream design and are treated as hard: a
single-detector front end, a fixed parameter budget, a self-supervised stage 1, strict
fold discipline, and no dependence on \texttt{ml4gw}. They are stated in full in
\texttt{CLAUDE.md} and motivated in \texttt{docs/improvement-plan.md}.

The parameter budget is enforced against measured counts read from the distributed
checkpoints rather than inferred from the published topology:

\section{Evaluation protocol}

Detection efficiency is quoted at a fixed false-alarm rate, with the sensitive volume
that implies. Background comes from time slides at lags well clear of the 11\,ms
inter-detector light-travel time, scored by the same selection object as the
foreground. Folds are grouped by GPS time; the evaluation fold is opened once, at the
end, and an audit trail records every access.

\section{Baseline reproduction}
\label{sec:repro}

The distributed weights are calibration-locked to the environment they were trained in:
the upstream \texttt{environment.yml} pins \texttt{torch}~1.12.1 with CUDA~11.2 and
states that a different build can shift the GPU forward pass and break the frozen
false-alarm-rate calibration. That build cannot be installed on the GPUs available to
us, since the CUDA~11.2 wheels predate the driver stack on the partition we run on, so
we use \texttt{torch}~2.6.0 built against CUDA~12.4. The upstream instruction for this
case is to match the pinned build as closely as the hardware allows and re-validate the
bundled recovery demonstration before quoting any false-alarm rate. We report that
re-validation here.

Running the bundled 256\,s segment containing GW190521 through the distributed weights
on a Tesla~V100, we recover

with per-detector significances $\sigma_{H_1}=5.54$ and $\sigma_{L_1}=5.35$, and a single
window above the $\sigma_\mathrm{net}>4$ threshold across the 253 windows of the segment.
The agreement is at the precision the reference values are quoted to.

Two caveats bound what this establishes. First, agreement on one loud event constrains
the forward pass in the regime where the signal is strong; a false-alarm rate is a
property of the noise tail, and the demonstration deliberately quotes none, since a
single short segment carries no time-slide livetime. Second, this validates the
\emph{inference} path only. The second half of the reproduction requirement, that a
reimplemented stage-1 and stage-2 training run recover score distributions and
downstream efficiency matching the distributed weights, requires strain beyond the
bundled segment and is not yet addressed.

We also record a portability defect encountered here, since it affects any new user of
the package. The repository commits nine symbolic links under \texttt{search\_mode/}
that point into the author's own scratch storage. On any other machine these dangle,
and \texttt{driver\_streams.py} calls \texttt{os.makedirs} with \texttt{exist\_ok=True}
at import time, which does not suppress \texttt{FileExistsError} when the path exists as
a dangling link rather than a directory. The documented demonstration therefore fails on
a fresh clone, in a way that resembles a local environment fault.

\section{Whitening: which upstream function the weights were built with}
\label{sec:whitening}

The upstream repository contains two whitening implementations, and they are not
equivalent. \texttt{improved/utilities.py::whiten} divides the Fourier transform by
$\sqrt{S(f) + 10^{-40}}$ and is used by \texttt{whiten\_batch} when preparing training
data. The deployed search does something else: \texttt{MassiveEventPipeline.\_whiten}
calls \texttt{whiten\_batch\_gwpy\_o1}, which builds a \textsc{gwpy}
\texttt{TimeSeries} and applies a time-domain FIR whitening filter against the
run-averaged reference ASD with \texttt{fduration}~$=2$~s and a 20~Hz high-pass, then
notches the calibration and mains lines with $Q=40$.

The distinction matters twice over. First, the additive $10^{-40}$ in the former is an
\emph{absolute} floor, while an O3a reference PSD spans $3\times10^{-51}$ to
$7\times10^{-40}$ and sits near $3\times10^{-47}$ at 100~Hz. The epsilon therefore
exceeds the physical PSD by roughly six orders of magnitude across the whole sensitive
band, and the division degenerates into a near-constant rescaling. The search path
instead floors at $10^{-10}$ times the PSD's own median, which touches no in-band bin.
Second, the FIR conditioning corrupts \texttt{fduration}/2~$=1$~s at each edge of the
window, which is precisely why the pipeline whitens 4~s and retains only the central
2~s of context.

We initially ported the former. On real O3a strain the resulting series correlates with
the correctly whitened one at $0.28$, and its band power is inverted: 19\% below 100~Hz
against 3\% for the search path, and 0.5\% between 100 and 500~Hz against 16\%. Every
tile built before this was corrected is therefore a time--frequency map of essentially
unwhitened, low-frequency-dominated strain, and the stage-1 comparison reported in an
earlier version of this note is withdrawn in full; its measured association between
reconstruction error and tile brightness was a property of that red-tilted input, not of
the model. Nothing in Section~\ref{sec:repro} is affected, because the reproduction gate
executes upstream's own code.

The reason this survived as long as it did is worth recording, because it is a
property of the representation rather than an accident. Rebuilding the tile bank from
the identical noise windows with the corrected whitening gives tiles whose per-pixel
correlation with the old ones has median $0.003$ (5th to 95th percentile $-0.038$ to
$+0.039$): the two banks share essentially nothing. Yet their aggregate statistics are
almost identical --- mean pixel value $0.300$ against $0.277$, pixel standard deviation
$0.169$ against $0.171$, no constant tiles in either, and a ratio of low-band to
high-band mean intensity of $0.99$ in both. The Q-transform is applied with
\texttt{norm="median"}, which normalises each frequency row by its own median and so
removes most of the spectral tilt that whitening establishes, and the subsequent
per-tile min--max normalisation removes the rest of the scale. Every aggregate check
available on the tiles is therefore nearly blind to whether the strain was whitened at
all, and every such check passed throughout.

Our implementation now agrees with \texttt{\_whiten} to a relative RMS difference of
$4\times10^{-8}$ on the retained 2~s, which is the float32 round-trip of upstream's own
output, and that agreement is asserted by a test that imports the vendored pipeline
rather than reimplementing it.

A third detail was found in passing. \texttt{improved\_pipeline} defines both O1 and O3a
calibration-line lists and an \texttt{infer\_line\_configuration()} that selects
\texttt{"o3a"} for an O3a preparation directory, but \texttt{\_whiten} passes the string
\texttt{"o1"} literally, so the O3a search notches the O1 calibration lines and the O1
L1 dither lines. We match the deployed behaviour by default, since matching the shipped
weights takes precedence over notching the physically appropriate lines, and treat the
O3a list as an ablation.

\section{What the distributed score responds to}
\label{sec:stage1}

With the whitening corrected, the tile bank was rebuilt (19\,991 training and 2002
validation tiles from the training fold of the O3a background set) and the
reimplemented stage-1 autoencoder retrained on the upstream recipe: Adam at $10^{-3}$,
weight decay $10^{-5}$, batch 64, \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau}(0.5, 5), ten epochs,
seed 42. Both that model and the distributed weights were then scored on the same 2002
held-out noise tiles, and each score distribution standardised to zero mean and unit
variance so that the comparison is of the tail that sets the false-alarm rate rather
than of an arbitrary normalisation.

The retrained model finds busy, bright tiles hard, which is what a reconstruction
objective should do: there is more structure to reproduce. The distributed model finds
\emph{dark, sparse} tiles hard, and does so almost deterministically --- a rank
correlation of $-0.95$ against the mean pixel value means the ordering that the shipped
per-detector significance imposes on noise tiles is very nearly reproduced by a single
scalar computed without reference to the network's 251\,394 parameters.

The mechanism is the weak-supervision margin acting on a min--max normalised input. The
margin is trained to raise reconstruction error on signal tiles, and after per-tile
min--max normalisation --- which sets the dynamic range from the loudest pixel --- a
signal tile is exactly a sparse bright track on an otherwise dark field. The injection
ladder of Section~\ref{sec:whitening} shows the same effect from the other side: adding
a louder signal to a fixed noise window drives the tile \emph{mean} monotonically down,
from $-0.054$ at network SNR 8 to $-0.163$ at SNR 40, because the injected chirp becomes
the pixel that sets the normalisation.

Two consequences, of different weight. First, and with the smaller weight: this is not
yet the reproduction requirement of Section~\ref{sec:repro}. The retrained model is
stage 1 and the distributed one is stage 2, so a sign reversal between them is expected
rather than anomalous, and the requirement cannot be assessed until the margin fine-tune
is reproduced --- which needs the injection campaign that
Section~\ref{sec:whitening} establishes is now available. Second, and independently of
any comparison: the $\rho = -0.95$ result is a statement about the distributed weights
alone. It says that a substantial part of the shipped score is a response to tile
sparsity rather than to any property specific to the noise distribution, and that
per-tile min--max normalisation is what makes sparsity and loudness two views of the
same quantity. That is direct support for both a tile-independent amplitude scaling
(R2) and a learned readout in place of reconstruction error (Phase 4), and it is
measured on the shipped model rather than argued from its architecture.

\section{The stage-2 margin fine-tune}
\label{sec:stage2}

\subsection*{What the released package makes reproducible}

The upstream README states that the vendored weights' ``training/calibration code and
data are not part of this release'', and the projected signal banks the injection stage
needs are likewise not shipped. The stage-2 \emph{procedure} is therefore reproducible
--- \texttt{improved\_pipeline.train\_margin\_model} is vendored in full --- while the
stage-2 \emph{artifact} is not, because neither the noise it was trained on nor the
waveform bank it was trained against is public. That distinction governs how everything
below should be read, and it means the reproduction requirement of
Section~\ref{sec:repro} cannot be closed by retraining. What can be, and has been,
closed is the inference path: the whitening agrees with the deployed function to
$4\times10^{-8}$, the weights load into the reimplemented topology with
\texttt{strict=True}, and the bundled demo recovers its event at the quoted net
$\sigma$.

\subsection*{Three properties of the margin that the paper does not fix}

Read off the source rather than the description:
\begin{itemize}
\item The margin is \textbf{multiplicative}: $\mathrm{relu}(m\,\overline{L_n} -
\overline{L_s})$, not $\mathrm{relu}(m + \overline{L_n} - \overline{L_s})$. Noise MSE
here is $\sim2\times10^{-3}$, so an additive reading with $m=3$ is saturated for every
batch that will ever exist --- the hinge never switches off and the gradient degenerates
to ``raise signal error without bound'', a term with no fixed point rather than a margin.
\item The noise term inside the hinge is \textbf{detached}. The margin may raise signal
error; it may not satisfy itself by lowering noise error.
\item It is a $\mathrm{relu}$ of the batch \textbf{means}, not the mean of per-tile
$\mathrm{relu}$s, so a batch already satisfying the margin on average contributes no
gradient even when individual tiles fall short.
\end{itemize}
Also not inferable from the paper: the fine-tuning learning rate is $10^{-3}$, the same
as stage 1 rather than reduced; no gradient clipping is applied;
\texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} is constructed with \texttt{threshold=1e-3} against Torch's
default of $10^{-4}$; and the checkpoint is selected on a \emph{detection} criterion
(validation injections above $\mu+3\sigma$ of the validation noise, tie-broken by mean
separation) while the learning-rate scheduler steps on the validation \emph{loss}.

\subsection*{The run}

Our stage-1 checkpoint was fine-tuned on a matched pair of banks: 19\,991 noise tiles
and 19\,991 injected tiles built from the \emph{same} windows, with IMRPhenomPv2 sources
drawn at network SNR uniform in $(8, 25)$ --- upstream's
\texttt{PROJECTED\_TARGET\_NETWORK\_SNR\_RANGE}, rescaled so that
$\sqrt{\rho_{H1}^2+\rho_{L1}^2}$ hits the target, matching
\texttt{maybe\_rescale\_projected\_signal\_pair}. Ten epochs, batch 64 per class, seed
42. The best checkpoint fell at epoch 8 of 10, against upstream's reported 9 of 10.

The fine-tune degrades noise reconstruction substantially and non-monotonically: the
validation noise mean rises from $1.9\times10^{-3}$ to $3.1\times10^{-2}$ after one
epoch, with the detection criterion collapsing to 4 of 1984, before recovering to
$7.8\times10^{-3}$ and 1416 of 1984 by epoch 8. That transient is the multiplicative
margin overwhelming the reconstruction term and then being pulled back by it, and it is
invisible in any summary that reports only the final loss.

Both models scored on 19\,994 held-out noise tiles and 2002 injections. Thresholds are
set on each model's \emph{own} noise distribution, so the two are quoted at equal
fractions of noise surviving --- the only scale-free comparison available. This is not a
false-alarm rate: that needs the time-slide background and the trials factor.

The margin moves our model firmly in the direction of the distributed one on every axis
and gets nowhere near it. Ten epochs of weak supervision on our injection population
takes the association with tile brightness from $+0.86$ to $+0.35$; the distributed model
sits at $-0.95$. Two readings are available and the data here cannot separate them: either

One structure in the joint distribution is worth recording because it explains the
$10^{-3}$ row. The two models' scores anticorrelate through the bulk but \emph{agree}
sharply on the extreme tail: the scatter carries a distinct high-score arm on which both
models rank the same tiles as hardest. Those tiles are what set each model's $99.9$th
percentile, so both thresholds are fixed by the same population of genuinely anomalous
noise --- presumably glitches --- rather than by anything either model learned about
signals. That is also the honest reason neither model passes injections at $10^{-3}$
here, and it is an argument for the glitch arm rather than against either front end.

Finally, a caveat that applies to every number in this section: the validation fold was
used to select our checkpoint, so it is held out for the distributed weights and not for
ours. Under constraint C4 the clean comparison surface is the quarantined evaluation
fold, and it stays quarantined until there is a result to report on it.

\section{Efficiency at fixed false-alarm rate, and what it says about the front end}
\label{sec:far}

\subsection*{The instrument}

Every number before this section was a diagnostic, because none was a false-alarm rate.
Building one required three things. First, a slice of the training fold that is neither
fitted to nor \emph{selected} against: the stage-2 checkpoint is chosen to maximise
detections above a threshold set by the validation noise, so the validation tail is
mildly optimised and a FAR quoted against it would be optimistic. The training fold now
splits three ways in GPS order --- fit 50\%, select 20\%, background 30\% --- and the
fold guard refuses to hand out the background split in any phase but calibration.
Costing the fit split 25 points is close to free, because it holds $\sim$1.4 million
distinct 4~s windows and a tile bank draws twenty thousand of them; the background is
limited by livetime and nothing else.

Second, a scan that pays for the Q-transform once. \texttt{scan\_background.py} writes
per-detector score \emph{time series} on a grid both detectors share, at 1~s stride so
that the 1~s crops the network sees are exactly non-overlapping. Time slides afterwards
are an array roll, which is what makes years of background affordable: 1.17~d of
coincident livetime becomes 6.39~yr at 2000 lags without touching strain again.
Triggers are clustered on 4~s, upstream's window --- without it a single glitch at 1~s
stride is counted four times --- and lags step by 4~s so that consecutive lags cannot
return the same trigger twice.

Third, a foreground measured through the identical path.
\texttt{scan\_injections.py} projects one source onto both detectors with a single
common amplitude scale, the correct antenna factors and the geocentre time delay, so the
H1/L1 relationship the ranking statistic reads is the one a real source would produce.
Two independently drawn single-detector injections would score well on a per-detector
arm and mean nothing coincident.

The ranking statistic is upstream's, read off \texttt{MassiveEventPipeline.\_fullmag}:
$\sigma_d = (L_d - \mu_d)/s_d$ per detector and
$\mathrm{net} = (\sigma_{H1} + \sigma_{L1})/\sqrt{2}$ --- the sum over $\sqrt{2}$, not
the quadrature sum.

\subsection*{The result}

8000 coincident injections, network SNR drawn uniformly in $(6, 40)$, against 6.39~yr of
time-slide background at a trials factor of 4:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
FAR [1/yr] & net $\sigma$ threshold & Efficiency & 68\% interval \\
\midrule
100 & 22.05 & $0.000$ & $[0.000, 0.000]$ \\
10  & 27.27 & $0.000$ & $[0.000, 0.000]$ \\
1   & 32.43 & $0.000$ & $[0.000, 0.000]$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Zero at every resolvable false-alarm rate, in every SNR bin from 6 to 40 and every total
mass bin from 20 to 240 $M_\odot$. The reason is a single number: the loudest injection
in the campaign reaches net $\sigma = 19.3$, and the background reaches $32.4$. There is
no threshold that admits any signal and excludes the noise.

The instrument is not broken, and that matters, so it is worth stating what was checked.
Injection net $\sigma$ responds monotonically to network SNR --- median $2.92$, $6.13$,
$8.28$, $10.35$ across SNR bins $(6,10)$, $(10,15)$, $(15,25)$, $(25,40)$ --- and 97.4\%
of injections raise the per-detector score above its noise mean, which is the direction
the margin fine-tune was trained to produce. The signal path works. It is simply
nowhere near the glitch tail.

\subsection*{What this measures, and what it does not}

It measures the raw front-end statistic \emph{with no vetoes applied}, and upstream
never claims that quantity is a search. The shipped calibration stores its FAR curves
under the names \texttt{far\_curve\_cond\_coh} and \texttt{far\_curve\_global\_coh} ---
both conditioned on coherence --- and the deployed pipeline additionally applies a
centroid/morphology cut, a glitch arm, two mass-specialist CNNs and a likelihood-ratio
cascade before quoting anything. What this section measures is therefore how the search's
discriminating power is divided between the autoencoder and everything downstream of it,
and the answer is stark: at a fixed FAR, essentially all of it is downstream.

That has a direct consequence for the improvement programme, and it is not the one the
plan assumed. Improving noise-versus-injection separation at the tile level --- which is
what the stage-2 margin optimises, and what the representation and readout changes of
Phases 3 and 4 target --- is \emph{not} the same as improving the search. The binding
constraint is the glitch tail, not signal sensitivity, and a change that sharpens
tile-level separation while leaving the glitch tail where it is buys exactly nothing at
fixed FAR. Any front-end change must therefore be evaluated after the vetoes, which
makes the coherence cut a prerequisite for the rest of the programme rather than a later
phase.

One caveat on the background itself. The 6.39~yr of slid livetime contains only 1.17~d
of distinct noise, so the deep tail is assembled from a small number of recurring loud
glitches paired against many different samples of the other detector. That is standard
practice, and it is what upstream does too, but with 1.17~d it is thin: upstream's
shipped calibration quotes 47.4~yr of background from a correspondingly larger stretch of
real data. The thresholds above should be read as resolving the shape of the tail, not
as a precise measurement of its extreme end.

\section{The vetoes, and where the efficiency is actually lost}
\label{sec:coherence}

Section~\ref{sec:far} measured the front end alone and found zero efficiency at every
resolvable false-alarm rate, and argued that the missing discrimination has to live in
the vetoes. It does. Adding them is the largest single change measured in this note.

\subsection*{What was added}

Two quantities and a branch, ported from \texttt{massive\_pipeline.py} with constants
from the shipped \texttt{massive\_calibration\_BA.json}, and checked against the
vendored methods themselves to $10^{-6}$:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Coherence}, band-limited to 20--500 Hz on the central 1~s and maximised
over $\pm 45$ samples: $\max_\tau |2\langle a, b_\tau\rangle| / (|a|^2 + |b|^2)$. The
symmetric norm is not a Pearson correlation, and the difference is the point: it is
maximised only when the two detectors carry comparable energy, so a loud glitch in one
against quiet data in the other --- the dominant background of a coincident search ---
scores near zero, where Pearson would normalise the mismatch away.
\item \textbf{Centroid}, the energy-weighted mean frequency of the central 0.5~s in
20--400 Hz, required below $f_\mathrm{cut} = 190.8$~Hz in both detectors.
\item \textbf{The branch}: a trigger passing both is ranked against a mass-conditioned
background, everything else against a general one. The shipped calibration stores
exactly this pair, as \texttt{far\_curve\_cond\_coh} and \texttt{far\_curve\_global\_coh}.
Pooling them would be a different and much weaker search.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{The result}

The veto is an extremely strong background rejection: of the 40.2 million clustered
time-slide triggers, \textbf{0.11\%} survive both cuts. Over 6.39~yr of background the
mass-conditioned channel retains 547 triggers above net $\sigma = 6$ against 500\,864 in
the general channel, and the threshold at a false-alarm rate of 100/yr falls from
$21.8$ to $\mathbf{8.2}$.

\subsection*{What each veto contributes}

The vetoes cost injections as well as background: only 25.7\% of sources reach the
mass-conditioned channel, and since all of the efficiency comes from that channel the
two numbers factor exactly as $0.187 = 0.257 \times 0.728$. It is tempting to read that
as a diagnosis --- three quarters of the signal is being thrown away, so recover it ---
and an earlier draft of this note did exactly that, on the strength of the centroid being
nearly mass-independent. Measuring it says the opposite.

\midrule
none (front end only) & $1.0000$ & $1.000$ & $0.000$ & $22.05$ \\
coherence only        & $0.0468$ & $0.703$ & $0.006$ & $13.44$ \\
morphology only       & $0.0412$ & $0.259$ & $0.000$ & $20.59$ \\
both (deployed)       & $0.0011$ & $0.257$ & $\mathbf{0.187}$ & $8.23$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

(efficiency at a false-alarm rate of 100/yr; threshold in net $\sigma$ at the same rate.)

Dropping the morphology gate costs a factor of thirty in efficiency, not gains one. The
gate discards 57\% of injections and 96\% of background, and it is the second number
that decides the trade. Judging a veto from its signal cost alone is not a
half-measurement, it is the wrong sign.

The pair combines about as well as a pair can. On background they are close to
independent --- $0.0468 \times 0.0412 = 0.0019$ against an observed $0.0011$, so they
reject largely different noise populations. On signal they are close to nested: 25.9\% of
injections pass the morphology gate and 99.2\% of those also pass coherence, so the
second cut costs almost nothing once the first has been applied. Independent on noise and
redundant on signal is exactly the property one wants from two vetoes.

The observation that motivated the wrong diagnosis survives, with a different meaning.
The centroid is nearly mass-independent on this population --- median $194.5$~Hz against
a $190.8$~Hz cut, and $194.9$, $195.1$, $194.2$~Hz across $20$--$60$, $60$--$100$ and
$100$--$240\,M_\odot$ --- so it is not selecting heavy mergers by their merger frequency,
which is the physical motivation the name suggests. Whitened noise band-limited to
20--400~Hz has an energy-weighted mean frequency near 210~Hz by construction, and in a
0.5~s window a source at these amplitudes is a minority of the band energy. But the gate
does not need to track the source to be useful: it is cutting on where the \emph{noise}
energy sits, which separates glitch classes, and empirically that is worth a factor of 24
in background. It is a noise-property cut wearing a signal-property name.

Whether a morphology statistic that did track the source could hold more signal at the
same background rejection remains open, and is a candidate change --- but it is a
question about improving something that works, not about repairing something broken.

\subsection*{Why 10/yr and 1/yr are still zero, and what would change it}

The binding constraint at the deeper rates has moved, and it is no longer the statistic.
The mass-conditioned channel holds 547 background triggers over 6.39~yr, so at a trials
factor of 4 the threshold at 10/yr is the 15th largest of them and at 1/yr it is the
single largest, $31.4$. A threshold set by one event is not a measurement. The loudest
injection reaches a false-alarm rate of $13.2$/yr, and the median at SNR 25--40 is
$1.1\times10^5$/yr, so the population is a factor of $\sim$13 from the 1/yr level rather
than orders of magnitude away.

Two things would move it, and they are different in kind. More lags would deepen the
quantile --- 20\,000 lags would put the 1/yr threshold at the 16th largest of $\sim$5500
triggers instead of the largest of 547 --- but they reuse the same 1.17~d of distinct
noise, so they buy resolution rather than information. More distinct background livetime
is what actually buys information, and this analysis uses 30\% of one fold of a
56-segment set. Upstream's shipped calibration quotes 47.4~yr from a correspondingly
larger stretch.

\section{Running the measurement on the distributed weights}
\label{sec:headtohead}

The distributed stage-2 weights were put through the identical chain --- same background
segments, same 7196-lag slide plan, same 8000 injections with the same drawn parameters,
same vetoes, same two-channel structure, each model thresholded on its own background.
The efficiency at a false-alarm rate of 100/yr came out at $0.240$ against $0.188$ for
our retrained model.

\textbf{That difference is not a result and should not be read as one.} The two models
share an architecture and differ in their training sets: ours saw twenty thousand tiles
from one fold of O3a, theirs saw a set that is not public and is certainly larger. A gap
in that direction was the expected outcome before the measurement was made, and nothing
in it distinguishes a deficiency of our reimplementation from the size of its training
set. Since the distributed weights cannot be reproduced from public data
(Section~\ref{sec:stage2}), the comparison cannot be made fair by any amount of further
work, and it is recorded here so that it is not mistaken for a baseline later. The
baseline against which changes are judged is our own model under a fixed protocol,
compared with itself.

Two things the run is good for.

\emph{The vetoes set a ceiling that no front end can exceed.} Above network SNR 25 the
two models are indistinguishable --- $0.306$ against $0.319$, then $0.333$ against
$0.335$ --- and that plateau is the fraction of injections the vetoes admit to the
mass-conditioned channel at those amplitudes. For loud sources the choice of front end
does not enter the answer at all. This is a property of the pipeline rather than of
either model, and it bounds what any front-end change can buy: whatever is gained has to
be gained at the quiet end, because the loud end is already saturated by something else.

\emph{The chain runs on a model it was not developed against.} The scan, the slides, the
vetoes and the efficiency calculation all produced sensible numbers on an independently
trained checkpoint. That is a software check, and worth having before any of it is used
to judge a change.

One tempting reading is explicitly \emph{not} supported: our background reaches net
$\sigma = 32.4$ where the distributed model's stops at $16.7$, which raises our threshold
and costs us the quiet injections. That is a correct description of the arithmetic, but
attributing it to a defect in our objective rather than to a model fitted on twenty
thousand tiles handling rare noise badly is not something these data can do. The
observation is kept as a hypothesis to test against our own seeds, not as a diagnosis.

\section{The baseline, and the bar a change has to clear}
\label{sec:baseline}

Three seeds of the identical recipe --- same tile banks, same injections, same schedule,
differing only in initialisation and batch order --- carried through the whole chain:
stage 1, stage 2, background scan, 7196 lags, vetoes, efficiency.

\textbf{Baseline: efficiency $0.196$ at a false-alarm rate of 100/yr, with three seeds
spanning $0.177$ to $0.222$. A change must move the number by more than $0.045$ before it
means anything.} All three seeds give exactly zero at 10/yr and 1/yr, so those rates
cannot discriminate between anything yet and are not usable as an outcome measure until
the background or the vetoes improve.

This is the first quantity in the project that can decide whether a change helped, and it
is worth being blunt about how coarse it is. A seed range of $0.045$ on a mean of $0.196$
is $\pm 12\%$, so only fairly large effects are detectable with three seeds. A change
expected to produce a few per cent will need more seeds to resolve, and that cost should
be budgeted before the change is run rather than discovered afterwards.

It also settles a comparison made earlier in this note. Section~\ref{sec:headtohead}
measured $0.188$ for one seed of our pipeline against $0.240$ for the distributed
weights, a difference of $0.052$ --- barely outside the $0.045$ that our own seeds span
among themselves. Even setting aside that the two models were trained on different data
and the comparison was never interpretable, a single-seed difference of that size would
not have been resolvable in any case. The seed spread had to exist before that number
could be read at all, and it did not.
