\begin{abstract}
Notes on a set of proposed machine-learning improvements to MADGRAV, a blind
gravitational-wave anomaly search \citep{Inguglia2026}. We reimplement the stage-1 and
stage-2 front end, which the upstream release does not distribute, and evaluate every
proposed change at fixed false-alarm rate against time-slide background pushed through
the same selection as the foreground.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and constraints}

Five constraints are inherited from the upstream design and are treated as hard: a
single-detector front end, a fixed parameter budget, a self-supervised stage 1, strict
fold discipline, and no dependence on \texttt{ml4gw}. They are stated in full in
\texttt{CLAUDE.md} and motivated in \texttt{docs/improvement-plan.md}.

The parameter budget is enforced against measured counts read from the distributed
checkpoints rather than inferred from the published topology:

\section{Evaluation protocol}

Detection efficiency is quoted at a fixed false-alarm rate, with the sensitive volume
that implies. Background comes from time slides at lags well clear of the 11\,ms
inter-detector light-travel time, scored by the same selection object as the
foreground. Folds are grouped by GPS time; the evaluation fold is opened once, at the
end, and an audit trail records every access.

\section{Baseline reproduction}
\label{sec:repro}

The distributed weights are calibration-locked to the environment they were trained in:
the upstream \texttt{environment.yml} pins \texttt{torch}~1.12.1 with CUDA~11.2 and
states that a different build can shift the GPU forward pass and break the frozen
false-alarm-rate calibration. That build cannot be installed on the GPUs available to
us, since the CUDA~11.2 wheels predate the driver stack on the partition we run on, so
we use \texttt{torch}~2.6.0 built against CUDA~12.4. The upstream instruction for this
case is to match the pinned build as closely as the hardware allows and re-validate the
bundled recovery demonstration before quoting any false-alarm rate. We report that
re-validation here.

Running the bundled 256\,s segment containing GW190521 through the distributed weights
on a Tesla~V100, we recover

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Quantity & Reference & Measured \\
\midrule
network significance $\sigma_\mathrm{net}$ & $\approx 7.7$ & $7.70$ \\
high-mass specialist $P(\mathrm{signal})$  & $\approx 0.99$ & $0.994$ \\
low-mass specialist $P(\mathrm{signal})$   & $\approx 0.95$ & $0.954$ \\
verdict & \textsc{recovered} & \textsc{recovered} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

with per-detector significances $\sigma_{H_1}=5.54$ and $\sigma_{L_1}=5.35$, and a single
window above the $\sigma_\mathrm{net}>4$ threshold across the 253 windows of the segment.
The agreement is at the precision the reference values are quoted to.

Two caveats bound what this establishes. First, agreement on one loud event constrains
the forward pass in the regime where the signal is strong; a false-alarm rate is a
property of the noise tail, and the demonstration deliberately quotes none, since a
single short segment carries no time-slide livetime. Second, this validates the
\emph{inference} path only. The second half of the reproduction requirement, that a
reimplemented stage-1 and stage-2 training run recover score distributions and
downstream efficiency matching the distributed weights, requires strain beyond the
bundled segment and is not yet addressed.

We also record a portability defect encountered here, since it affects any new user of
the package. The repository commits nine symbolic links under \texttt{search\_mode/}
that point into the author's own scratch storage. On any other machine these dangle,
and \texttt{driver\_streams.py} calls \texttt{os.makedirs} with \texttt{exist\_ok=True}
at import time, which does not suppress \texttt{FileExistsError} when the path exists as
a dangling link rather than a directory. The documented demonstration therefore fails on
a fresh clone, in a way that resembles a local environment fault.

\section{Whitening: which upstream function the weights were built with}
\label{sec:whitening}

The upstream repository contains two whitening implementations, and they are not
equivalent. \texttt{improved/utilities.py::whiten} divides the Fourier transform by
$\sqrt{S(f) + 10^{-40}}$ and is used by \texttt{whiten\_batch} when preparing training
data. The deployed search does something else: \texttt{MassiveEventPipeline.\_whiten}
calls \texttt{whiten\_batch\_gwpy\_o1}, which builds a \textsc{gwpy}
\texttt{TimeSeries} and applies a time-domain FIR whitening filter against the
run-averaged reference ASD with \texttt{fduration}~$=2$~s and a 20~Hz high-pass, then
notches the calibration and mains lines with $Q=40$.

The distinction matters twice over. First, the additive $10^{-40}$ in the former is an
\emph{absolute} floor, while an O3a reference PSD spans $3\times10^{-51}$ to
$7\times10^{-40}$ and sits near $3\times10^{-47}$ at 100~Hz. The epsilon therefore
exceeds the physical PSD by roughly six orders of magnitude across the whole sensitive
band, and the division degenerates into a near-constant rescaling. The search path
instead floors at $10^{-10}$ times the PSD's own median, which touches no in-band bin.
Second, the FIR conditioning corrupts \texttt{fduration}/2~$=1$~s at each edge of the
window, which is precisely why the pipeline whitens 4~s and retains only the central
2~s of context.

We initially ported the former. On real O3a strain the resulting series correlates with
the correctly whitened one at $0.28$, and its band power is inverted: 19\% below 100~Hz
against 3\% for the search path, and 0.5\% between 100 and 500~Hz against 16\%. Every
tile built before this was corrected is therefore a time--frequency map of essentially
unwhitened, low-frequency-dominated strain, and the stage-1 comparison reported in an
earlier version of this note is withdrawn in full; its measured association between
reconstruction error and tile brightness was a property of that red-tilted input, not of
the model. Nothing in Section~\ref{sec:repro} is affected, because the reproduction gate
executes upstream's own code.

The reason this survived as long as it did is worth recording, because it is a
property of the representation rather than an accident. Rebuilding the tile bank from
the identical noise windows with the corrected whitening gives tiles whose per-pixel
correlation with the old ones has median $0.003$ (5th to 95th percentile $-0.038$ to
$+0.039$): the two banks share essentially nothing. Yet their aggregate statistics are
almost identical --- mean pixel value $0.300$ against $0.277$, pixel standard deviation
$0.169$ against $0.171$, no constant tiles in either, and a ratio of low-band to
high-band mean intensity of $0.99$ in both. The Q-transform is applied with
\texttt{norm="median"}, which normalises each frequency row by its own median and so
removes most of the spectral tilt that whitening establishes, and the subsequent
per-tile min--max normalisation removes the rest of the scale. Every aggregate check
available on the tiles is therefore nearly blind to whether the strain was whitened at
all, and every such check passed throughout.

Our implementation now agrees with \texttt{\_whiten} to a relative RMS difference of
$4\times10^{-8}$ on the retained 2~s, which is the float32 round-trip of upstream's own
output, and that agreement is asserted by a test that imports the vendored pipeline
rather than reimplementing it.

A third detail was found in passing. \texttt{improved\_pipeline} defines both O1 and O3a
calibration-line lists and an \texttt{infer\_line\_configuration()} that selects
\texttt{"o3a"} for an O3a preparation directory, but \texttt{\_whiten} passes the string
\texttt{"o1"} literally, so the O3a search notches the O1 calibration lines and the O1
L1 dither lines. We match the deployed behaviour by default, since matching the shipped
weights takes precedence over notching the physically appropriate lines, and treat the
O3a list as an ablation.

\section{What the distributed score responds to}
\label{sec:stage1}

With the whitening corrected, the tile bank was rebuilt (19\,991 training and 2002
validation tiles from the training fold of the O3a background set) and the
reimplemented stage-1 autoencoder retrained on the upstream recipe: Adam at $10^{-3}$,
weight decay $10^{-5}$, batch 64, \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau}(0.5, 5), ten epochs,
seed 42. Both that model and the distributed weights were then scored on the same 2002
held-out noise tiles, and each score distribution standardised to zero mean and unit
variance so that the comparison is of the tail that sets the false-alarm rate rather
than of an arbitrary normalisation.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Standardised score & Retrained (stage 1) & Distributed (stage 2) \\
\midrule
median            & $-0.13$ & $-0.18$ \\
99th percentile   & $+2.49$ & $+3.31$ \\
99.9th percentile & $+3.92$ & $+6.08$ \\
maximum           & $+18.16$ & $+9.71$ \\
skewness          & $+3.64$ & $+2.00$ \\
excess kurtosis   & $+55.1$ & $+9.26$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The two models disagree about which tiles are difficult, and strongly: their per-tile
errors have a Spearman rank correlation of $-0.804$.

Ranking each model's error against elementary properties of the tile explains that
disagreement completely, and yields the more consequential measurement:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Tile property (Spearman $\rho$ with error) & Retrained & Distributed \\
\midrule
mean pixel value             & $+0.86$ & $-0.95$ \\
pixel standard deviation     & $+0.91$ & $-0.87$ \\
fraction of pixels above 0.5 & $+0.90$ & $-0.94$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The retrained model finds busy, bright tiles hard, which is what a reconstruction
objective should do: there is more structure to reproduce. The distributed model finds
\emph{dark, sparse} tiles hard, and does so almost deterministically --- a rank
correlation of $-0.95$ against the mean pixel value means the ordering that the shipped
per-detector significance imposes on noise tiles is very nearly reproduced by a single
scalar computed without reference to the network's 251\,394 parameters.

The mechanism is the weak-supervision margin acting on a min--max normalised input. The
margin is trained to raise reconstruction error on signal tiles, and after per-tile
min--max normalisation --- which sets the dynamic range from the loudest pixel --- a
signal tile is exactly a sparse bright track on an otherwise dark field. The injection
ladder of Section~\ref{sec:whitening} shows the same effect from the other side: adding
a louder signal to a fixed noise window drives the tile \emph{mean} monotonically down,
from $-0.054$ at network SNR 8 to $-0.163$ at SNR 40, because the injected chirp becomes
the pixel that sets the normalisation.

Two consequences, of different weight. First, and with the smaller weight: this is not
yet the reproduction requirement of Section~\ref{sec:repro}. The retrained model is
stage 1 and the distributed one is stage 2, so a sign reversal between them is expected
rather than anomalous, and the requirement cannot be assessed until the margin fine-tune
is reproduced --- which needs the injection campaign that
Section~\ref{sec:whitening} establishes is now available. Second, and independently of
any comparison: the $\rho = -0.95$ result is a statement about the distributed weights
alone. It says that a substantial part of the shipped score is a response to tile
sparsity rather than to any property specific to the noise distribution, and that
per-tile min--max normalisation is what makes sparsity and loudness two views of the
same quantity. That is direct support for both a tile-independent amplitude scaling
(R2) and a learned readout in place of reconstruction error (Phase 4), and it is
measured on the shipped model rather than argued from its architecture.

\section{The stage-2 margin fine-tune}
\label{sec:stage2}

\subsection*{What the released package makes reproducible}

The upstream README states that the vendored weights' ``training/calibration code and
data are not part of this release'', and the projected signal banks the injection stage
needs are likewise not shipped. The stage-2 \emph{procedure} is therefore reproducible
--- \texttt{improved\_pipeline.train\_margin\_model} is vendored in full --- while the
stage-2 \emph{artifact} is not, because neither the noise it was trained on nor the
waveform bank it was trained against is public. That distinction governs how everything
below should be read, and it means the reproduction requirement of
Section~\ref{sec:repro} cannot be closed by retraining. What can be, and has been,
closed is the inference path: the whitening agrees with the deployed function to
$4\times10^{-8}$, the weights load into the reimplemented topology with
\texttt{strict=True}, and the bundled demo recovers its event at the quoted net
$\sigma$.

\subsection*{Three properties of the margin that the paper does not fix}

Read off the source rather than the description:
\begin{itemize}
\item The margin is \textbf{multiplicative}: $\mathrm{relu}(m\,\overline{L_n} -
\overline{L_s})$, not $\mathrm{relu}(m + \overline{L_n} - \overline{L_s})$. Noise MSE
here is $\sim2\times10^{-3}$, so an additive reading with $m=3$ is saturated for every
batch that will ever exist --- the hinge never switches off and the gradient degenerates
to ``raise signal error without bound'', a term with no fixed point rather than a margin.
\item The noise term inside the hinge is \textbf{detached}. The margin may raise signal
error; it may not satisfy itself by lowering noise error.
\item It is a $\mathrm{relu}$ of the batch \textbf{means}, not the mean of per-tile
$\mathrm{relu}$s, so a batch already satisfying the margin on average contributes no
gradient even when individual tiles fall short.
\end{itemize}
Also not inferable from the paper: the fine-tuning learning rate is $10^{-3}$, the same
as stage 1 rather than reduced; no gradient clipping is applied;
\texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} is constructed with \texttt{threshold=1e-3} against Torch's
default of $10^{-4}$; and the checkpoint is selected on a \emph{detection} criterion
(validation injections above $\mu+3\sigma$ of the validation noise, tie-broken by mean
separation) while the learning-rate scheduler steps on the validation \emph{loss}.

\subsection*{The run}

Our stage-1 checkpoint was fine-tuned on a matched pair of banks: 19\,991 noise tiles
and 19\,991 injected tiles built from the \emph{same} windows, with IMRPhenomPv2 sources
drawn at network SNR uniform in $(8, 25)$ --- upstream's
\texttt{PROJECTED\_TARGET\_NETWORK\_SNR\_RANGE}, rescaled so that
$\sqrt{\rho_{H1}^2+\rho_{L1}^2}$ hits the target, matching
\texttt{maybe\_rescale\_projected\_signal\_pair}. Ten epochs, batch 64 per class, seed
42. The best checkpoint fell at epoch 8 of 10, against upstream's reported 9 of 10.

The fine-tune degrades noise reconstruction substantially and non-monotonically: the
validation noise mean rises from $1.9\times10^{-3}$ to $3.1\times10^{-2}$ after one
epoch, with the detection criterion collapsing to 4 of 1984, before recovering to
$7.8\times10^{-3}$ and 1416 of 1984 by epoch 8. That transient is the multiplicative
margin overwhelming the reconstruction term and then being pulled back by it, and it is
invisible in any summary that reports only the final loss.

\subsection*{Comparison against the distributed weights}

Both models scored on 19\,994 held-out noise tiles and 2002 injections. Thresholds are
set on each model's \emph{own} noise distribution, so the two are quoted at equal
fractions of noise surviving --- the only scale-free comparison available. This is not a
false-alarm rate: that needs the time-slide background and the trials factor.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Efficiency at a noise false-positive fraction of & Retrained (stage 2) & Distributed \\
\midrule
$10^{-1}$ & $0.872$ & $\mathbf{0.959}$ \\
$10^{-2}$ & $0.711$ & $\mathbf{0.879}$ \\
$10^{-3}$ & $0.034$ & $0.000$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At both operating points where the threshold is well determined the distributed weights
are clearly better, by 9 and 17 percentage points. We do not read this as a measurement
of reimplementation quality in either direction: our model saw 20\,000 tiles from one
O3a fold and our own injection population, and the distributed model saw neither, so
each is out of the other's training distribution and the comparison is confounded in
both directions at once.

The $10^{-3}$ row is reported only to be disowned. On the 2002-tile validation bank used
during training, the same quantities read $0.551$ and $0.224$ --- a factor of sixteen
higher for one model and unboundedly higher for the other --- because at 2002 samples the
$99.9$th percentile \emph{is} the second-largest observed value. The background sample
size sets how deep a tail may be quoted at all, and nothing about the first pair of
numbers announced that it was noise. This is the concrete form of the project's rule that
the currency is efficiency at fixed FAR: quoting it requires the background to support
the quantile.

\subsection*{What the margin did and did not move}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Spearman $\rho$ of error with & Retrained (stage 1) & Retrained (stage 2) & Distributed \\
\midrule
mean pixel value             & $+0.86$ & $+0.35$ & $-0.95$ \\
pixel standard deviation     & $+0.91$ & $+0.46$ & $-0.88$ \\
fraction of pixels above 0.5 & $+0.90$ & $+0.40$ & $-0.94$ \\
\midrule
rank correlation with the distributed model & $-0.804$ & $-0.279$ & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The margin moves our model firmly in the direction of the distributed one on every axis
and gets nowhere near it. Ten epochs of weak supervision on our injection population
takes the association with tile brightness from $+0.86$ to $+0.35$; the distributed model
sits at $-0.95$. Two readings are available and the data here cannot separate them: either
the shipped weights received far more weak supervision than ten epochs of this population,
or their $\rho=-0.95$ is a property of a training set we do not have. Either way, that
correlation is not something the published recipe produces on public data in the published
number of epochs.

One structure in the joint distribution is worth recording because it explains the
$10^{-3}$ row. The two models' scores anticorrelate through the bulk but \emph{agree}
sharply on the extreme tail: the scatter carries a distinct high-score arm on which both
models rank the same tiles as hardest. Those tiles are what set each model's $99.9$th
percentile, so both thresholds are fixed by the same population of genuinely anomalous
noise --- presumably glitches --- rather than by anything either model learned about
signals. That is also the honest reason neither model passes injections at $10^{-3}$
here, and it is an argument for the glitch arm rather than against either front end.

Finally, a caveat that applies to every number in this section: the validation fold was
used to select our checkpoint, so it is held out for the distributed weights and not for
ours. Under constraint C4 the clean comparison surface is the quarantined evaluation
fold, and it stays quarantined until there is a result to report on it.
